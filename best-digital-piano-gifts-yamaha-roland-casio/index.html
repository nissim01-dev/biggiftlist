How to pick the right digital piano

Start with who's playing and where it goes:

  • Just curious: the Alesis Recital is a budget pick with 88 keys. Taking lessons? Go straight to fully weighted keys.
  • Beginner taking lessons: Roland FP-10, Casio CDP-S160 or the Yamaha P-143 bundle with stand and bench.
  • Small space: the slim Casio Privia PX-S1100 or the compact Yamaha with Bluetooth.
  • Living room piano: a console like the Yamaha Arius YDP-105 or YDP-165, or the Casio PX-870, with three pedals and a cabinet.
  • Check what's in the box: many portables need a stand, bench and pedal bought separately. Bundles and consoles include them.
Gift tip: add a pair of closed-back headphones so they can practice at night without waking the house.

FAQ

What is the best digital piano for a beginner?

The Roland FP-10 and Casio CDP-S160 are popular first weighted pianos. For an all-in-one setup, the Yamaha P-143 bundle includes a stand and bench.

Weighted or semi-weighted keys?

Fully weighted (hammer action) keys feel like an acoustic piano and are what teachers recommend for lessons. Semi-weighted keys are lighter and fine for casual playing.

Portable or console digital piano?

Portables are slim and easy to move but often need a separate stand and pedals. Consoles have a cabinet, three pedals and usually better speakers, but they're heavy.

Can you play a digital piano with headphones?

Yes. All the pianos here have a headphone jack, and several have two, so a teacher and student can listen together.
