Launch monitor, rangefinder or GPS watch: which golf gift?

  • Launch monitor: for the golfer who practices a lot or wants to play simulator golf at home. It measures swing and ball data; it does not tell distances on the course.
  • Laser rangefinder: exact distance to the flag on the course. Best for golfers who care about precise yardages. Pick a model with a slope switch so it stays tournament-legal.
  • GPS watch: front, middle and back distances on the wrist without pulling anything out of the bag. Great for walkers and casual golfers.
  • Watch for subscriptions: simulator courses on the Garmin R10, some Garmin watch features and Arccos stats need a paid membership after the trial. Mention it in the card so it's not a surprise.
  • Home setup: a launch monitor indoors needs a net or screen and some room behind the ball. Check ceiling height and space first.
Gift tip: if you're not sure what they already own, a launch monitor is the safest big golf gift: few golfers have one, and it gets used all winter when courses are closed.

FAQ

What is the best big gift for a golfer?

A portable launch monitor like the Garmin Approach R10 is the most-wanted big golf gift. A premium rangefinder or a GPS watch is the best pick for golfers who mostly play rather than practice.

Rangefinder or GPS watch: which is better?

A laser rangefinder gives the exact distance to the flag; a GPS watch gives quick front, middle and back distances on your wrist. Many golfers end up using both.

Do golf launch monitors need a subscription?

Basic swing data on the Garmin R10 is included. Playing virtual courses in the Garmin Golf app needs a membership; other monitors vary.

Are slope rangefinders legal in tournaments?

Slope must be turned off for most competitions. Models with an external slope switch, like the Callaway 300 Pro and Bushnell Shift models, make that easy.
