How to pick the right juicer gift

Match the juicer to how they actually juice:

  • Green juice and leafy greens: a cold press (masticating) juicer like the Hurom H320 or Omega VSJ843 squeezes slowly and handles kale and celery better than a fast centrifugal juicer.
  • Hate chopping: a self-feeding hopper, like on the Hurom H400 or Kuvings AUTO9, takes whole apples and carrots and juices hands-free.
  • Hate scrubbing: Hurom's strainer-free Easy Clean models (H400, H70, H310A) skip the mesh screen that takes the longest to clean.
  • Want juice fast: a centrifugal juicer like the Breville Juice Fountain Cold makes a big jug in minutes, but it's louder and best for hard fruits and vegetables.
  • First juicer on a budget: the Ninja NeverClog cold press or Breville Juice Fountain Plus are easy starters.
  • Check the counter: self-feeding juicers are tall, so measure the space under their cabinets before you buy.
Gift tip: wrap the juicer with a set of glass bottles and a bag of their favorite citrus or a green juice recipe card, so the first glass happens on Christmas morning.

FAQ

What is the best juicer to give as a gift?

For a splurge, the Hurom H400: whole produce goes in and there's no mesh strainer to scrub. For a budget gift, the Ninja NeverClog cold press juicer is a well-reviewed starter.

Cold press or centrifugal juicer?

Cold press (masticating) juicers squeeze slowly, run quieter and handle leafy greens well, but cost more and take longer. Centrifugal juicers like the Breville Juice Fountain are faster and cheaper, and best for apples, carrots and other hard produce.

Is a $500+ juicer worth it?

For someone who juices most days, the time saved by a self-feeding hopper and easy cleanup is what keeps the juicer in use. For occasional juicers, a $150–$200 model is usually enough.

Hurom or Kuvings?

Both make premium slow juicers. Hurom's Easy Clean models skip the mesh strainer, while the Kuvings AUTO9 has an Auto-Cut hopper that slices and self-feeds whole produce.

Can a blender replace a juicer?

Not exactly. A blender keeps all the fiber and makes smoothies. A juicer separates the juice from the pulp. The Breville 3X Bluicer Pro does both from one base.
