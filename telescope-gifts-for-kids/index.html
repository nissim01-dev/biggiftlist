Buying a telescope for a child: quick tips

  • Weight and height: a scope a child can carry and reach without a step stool gets used far more.
  • Start with the Moon: it's bright, easy to find and stunning in any telescope. Planets come next.
  • Skip extreme magnification: high-power eyepieces make images dim and shaky. Lower power is easier for kids.
  • Supervise the Sun rule: never point a telescope at the Sun without a certified solar filter.
  • Smart telescopes for teens: they're pricier, but the instant photos are perfect for social-media-savvy teens.
Make it an event: wrap the telescope with a Moon calendar and plan a "first light" night for the first clear evening after Christmas.

FAQ

What age is good for a first telescope?

Most kids can enjoy a simple telescope from about age 6–8 with adult help. From around 10, kids can usually set up and aim a scope themselves.

What can kids see with a 70mm telescope?

The Moon's craters in great detail, Jupiter and its four largest moons, Saturn's rings as a small but clear shape, and bright star clusters.

Is a smart telescope good for a child?

For teens who like gadgets, yes. For younger kids, a simple refractor teaches them to find objects themselves and is far cheaper.

Are cheap telescopes for kids worth it?

Under-$100 scopes like the Gskyer 70mm or Celestron Travel Scope 70 are fine for the Moon and bright planets, which is exactly what younger kids enjoy most.
